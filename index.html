How it works

Free goods make each unit cheaper than its sticker price. If you buy 10 and get 1 free, you pay for 10 but hold 11, so your effective purchase is price × bought ÷ (bought + free) per unit.

Tax is added to that effective cost only when it is a real cost to you. If you are not registered (or cannot claim input tax), GST / VAT is money you never get back, so it is included in landed cost. If you are registered and the tax is recoverable as input tax credit, it is excluded — you reclaim it, so it is not part of your cost. Pick the toggle that matches your registration.

Your landed cost is then effective purchase + tax (when it is a cost) + freight + other cost per unit. That is also your break-even: sell below it and you lose money on every unit.

Two ways to read profit: margin on selling price is (price − landed) ÷ price — the share of each sale you keep. Markup on cost is (price − landed) ÷ landed — how much you add on top of cost. Markup is always the bigger number; a 25% margin is a 33% markup.

The suggested price works backwards from a target margin on the selling price: landed ÷ (1 − target%).

Assumptions & method
  • Free goods are modelled as a lower effective unit cost, not as extra revenue.
  • GST / VAT is a flat percentage of the effective purchase price only (freight and other cost are entered tax-inclusive).
  • Recoverable GST / VAT (input tax credit) is excluded from landed cost; non-recoverable tax is included.
  • Margin % is measured on the selling price; markup % is measured on cost.
  • Suggested price uses target margin on the selling price, valid for a target below 100%.
  • All amounts are per unit, in the currency you select. Empty fields count as zero; units bought defaults to 1.
Whether GST / VAT is a cost depends on your registration — pick the toggle that matches you. This is a planning estimate, not tax or accounting advice; confirm rates and input-tax eligibility with your invoice and a qualified adviser.
